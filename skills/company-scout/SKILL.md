# Company Scout

Not every company name tells you what you need to know. "Series A fintech startup" and "20-year-old outsourcing shop" can both look identical on a LinkedIn profile. This skill exists to close that gap — for one company at a time, quickly, without turning it into a research project.

## When to Use This

Trigger a company deep-dive when:
- The company name doesn't clearly indicate what it does
- You can't tell if it's a product company, a services/consulting shop, an outsourcer, or an enterprise
- The market or domain it serves is unclear
- A candidate's title or scope can't be judged without knowing the company's stage and structure
- Fit for a startup-oriented search depends on whether the environment is actually startup-like

Don't research every company. Only research the ones where the answer actually changes your decision.

## What to Establish

Gather only what you need, not everything that exists:

- **What does the company actually do?** Product category or service category, in one or two sentences.
- **Industry and domain** — fintech, healthtech, security, dev tools, whatever applies.
- **Stage and scale** — pre-seed, seed, Series A/B/C, growth, public, or an R&D center of a larger company. Rough headcount.
- **Environment type** — product company vs. services/outsourcing/consulting. This one distinction changes almost every downstream judgment about a candidate.
- **Engineering or business complexity** — is this the kind of environment where the role in question would carry real ownership, or is it likely narrow and process-heavy?

## How to Judge Fit Once You Know the Company

Positive signals for a startup or product-driven search:
- Product company, not a services shop
- Modern tech stack for the domain
- Evidence of shipping and iterating quickly
- Career progression is possible and visible for people who join

Negative signals:
- Legacy technology with little sign of modernization
- Heavy outsourcing, consulting, or staffing structure
- Long employee tenures with no visible progression (can indicate stagnation)
- Maintenance-only engineering work

Don't use brand prestige as a shortcut. A well-known name doesn't tell you whether the specific team or role has real ownership. Judge the actual environment.

## Rules

- Separate facts from interpretation. "They raised a Series B in [year]" is a fact. "This means the team is probably scaling fast" is an interpretation — label it as one.
- If you can't find enough to judge confidently, say so and decide conservatively rather than guessing. A weak guess dressed up as a fact is worse than an honest "unclear."
- Keep the write-up short. Three to five lines is usually enough to unblock a screening decision.

## Output

A short block, ready to drop into a screening note or a target-company list:

```
Company: [name]
Does: [one-line description]
Domain: [industry/category]
Stage/Scale: [stage, rough headcount]
Environment: [product / services / consulting / outsourcing / enterprise R&D]
Relevance: [why this matters for the candidate or search in question]
Confidence: [High / Medium / Low — and why, if Low]
```

Feed this into the **Candidate Screener** skill when it's blocking a GO/NO GO decision, or into **Market Researcher**'s target company list when it's about sourcing strategy rather than one candidate.
