# Israeli Tech Market

A general model screens an Israeli profile like an American one. It counts army years as job years, treats every college as a university, misses that "Annapurna Labs" is Amazon, and drops half the market because the location says only "Israel". This skill fixes that.

Two kinds of content live here. **Facts** (what a unit is, who acquired whom) you can rely on. **Our defaults** are how our team screens; they are not a market standard. Agree them with the hiring manager before your agent uses them. The role's own rules always win. Employer type or unit name alone is never grounds for a decision: judge what the person actually did.

## Location: Read It the Way Profiles Write It

LinkedIn profiles show Israeli locations in a few forms: a city ("Herzliya"), a district ("Tel Aviv District", "Center District", "Haifa District", "Jerusalem District", "North District", "South District"), or only "Israel".

- **Don't drop profiles that say only "Israel".** In an internal check (August 2026), 47% of about 67,000 Israeli profiles in our database had no district recorded. A search that requires a city or district never sees them. Where they live is unknown, so don't assume either way: reject on location only when the profile *clearly* names a place outside commuting range for this role.
- **The center.** Tel Aviv, Ramat Gan, Givatayim, Holon, Bat Yam, Herzliya, Ra'anana, Kfar Saba, Hod HaSharon, Netanya, Petah Tikva, Rosh HaAyin, Rehovot, Ness Ziona, Rishon LeZion, Modi'in: include all of them in the first search. Confirm the real commute and the office days with the candidate.
- **Haifa is not "far" for every role.** Haifa and Yokneam have their own large R&D cluster (chips, hardware, cloud infrastructure). For a hardware or chip role in Haifa, that crowd is the core pool. For a Tel Aviv startup, ask whether the client accepts a hybrid commute from Haifa.
- **Jerusalem and Be'er Sheva** have real tech scenes of their own. Ask, don't assume.
- **Don't use the employer's office as proof of where someone lives.** Many people work hybrid or remote.

## Army Service: Judge the Work, Not the Unit

Most Israeli engineers served, and many started their careers in the army. Read army service carefully, in both directions.

**Our team's default (not a market standard; agree it with the client):**
- Relevant **technical** army work counts at **half weight** toward years of experience. Six years as an army programmer counts as three.
- Army experience **alone is never enough.** We also require a minimum of civilian years, agreed per role (often the same as the role's experience bar).
- Non-technical service (combat, logistics, admin) doesn't count toward technical years. It's not a negative either.
- **A unit name proves nothing on its own.** Read what the person actually did there.
- Some roles count relevant army work fully (security, some hardware roles). Agree it with the client.

**Units, programs and training backgrounds worth a closer look** (a possible quality signal, never a substitute for civilian years; attending one doesn't prove later work): 8200, Unit 81, Mamram, the Talpiot program, Matzov, Ofek (324), Lotem, the Intelligence Corps programming course, Basmach (the IDF School of Computer Professions and Cyber Defense), Air Force and Navy cyber and software units.

**Age and timing.** Israelis usually start university after the army, around 21-23, and graduate around 25-27. A 27-year-old with two years of industry experience is on a normal track, not a slow one.

**Reserve duty (miluim).** Many Israelis have served long reserve periods since October 2023. A gap, a slow year, or a short role since then can be reserve duty. Never treat it as a negative, and never ask about it for screening. Israeli law forbids discrimination because of reserve service.

## Education: Universities, Colleges, Courses

- **Research universities:** Technion, Tel Aviv University (TAU), Hebrew University (HUJI), Ben-Gurion University (BGU), Bar-Ilan, University of Haifa, Weizmann Institute (graduate degrees only), Reichman University (formerly IDC Herzliya), Ariel University.
- **The Open University** gives full university degrees with open admission. A degree from it meets a "degree required" bar.
- **Colleges** (Afeka, HIT, SCE, Shenkar, Ruppin, Braude, Azrieli/JCE and others) give real degrees. A college degree meets a "degree required" bar. It's not an excellence signal on its own.
- **Training courses** (bootcamps, private programming courses) are not a degree and not work experience.
- **When a role asks for "a top university"**, get the exact list from the client for that role. Many clients mean Technion, TAU, Hebrew U and Weizmann; some add BGU and Bar-Ilan. Don't decide alone.

## Employers: What an Israeli Company Name Tells You

**Multinational R&D centers.** Intel, Microsoft, Google, Apple, Amazon/AWS, Nvidia, Meta, Qualcomm, Broadcom, Marvell, Salesforce, PayPal and many more run big engineering centers in Israel. These are real product engineering jobs, often senior. Judge the team, not the logo.

**Acquired Israeli startups.** People often still list the old name, so a model may not see that two entries are one employer:
- Mellanox → Nvidia · Annapurna Labs → Amazon (AWS) · Habana Labs → Intel · Siemplify → Google · Waze → Google · Moovit → Mobileye · Talon → Palo Alto Networks
- Mobileye is a separate public company, majority-owned by Intel. A move between Intel and Mobileye is a real job change.
- Don't count a rename after an acquisition as a job hop. Check the dates and whether the role continued before you combine two entries into one.

**Israeli product companies.** Large ones include Wix, monday.com, JFrog, Fiverr, Payoneer, Riskified, Check Point and NICE. Some big Israeli companies changed owners recently (for example, deals for Wiz with Google and CyberArk with Palo Alto Networks were announced in 2025), so check the current owner. In the older, larger vendors, teams range from new products to maintenance of mature ones. Judge the specific team, product and recency.

**Defense industry.** Elbit, Rafael, Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI): serious engineering, often excellent for hardware, embedded, chips and verification. For a fast SaaS startup role, check the fit carefully: many candidates can't describe their work in detail, and the stack may differ. Judge what the person actually built.

**Services and outsourcing houses.** Large IT services and project houses place their people at clients. Check who the real client was and what they built. Many of our clients see a long career only inside these houses as a weaker fit for product startup roles. Judge the actual projects.

**Banks, insurance and government IT:** judge the team and the stack. Many startup clients prefer product-company experience.

**Small market, small world.** The same strong engineers get many messages. The client's customers, partners and investors' portfolio companies are often off-limits. Get the do-not-poach list in writing on day one.

## Names and Languages

- **Hebrew names have many English spellings:** Itzhak / Yitzhak / Itzik, Yehuda / Yuda, Tzvi / Zvi / Tsvi, Moshe / Moishe, Haim / Chaim. Never match people by name. Use the LinkedIn profile URL.
- **Nicknames are common on profiles** (Itzik for Yitzhak, Dudi for David, Avi for Avraham). The name in the profile URL and the display name may differ.
- **Parts of a profile may be in Hebrew.** Read them. Don't treat Hebrew text as missing data.

## Outreach Tone in Israel

- Israeli tech people expect short, direct messages. First name, no "Dear", no long introduction.
- English is fine for tech roles. Hebrew can feel warmer for some roles; agree it with the client and the sender.
- Many senders are not native English speakers. Write plain, literal English with no idioms, so the message sounds like the person who sends it.
- Don't mention army units in an opener. It reads as profiling, and it says nothing about the person's work today.

## How to Use This With the Other Skills

- **Talent Sourcer:** include profiles with "Israel" only, add Hebrew name variants when you search for a known person, and run a past-employer angle on acquired startups under both names.
- **Candidate Screener:** apply the army, education and employer rules above before the general hard filters.
- **Company Scout:** check acquisitions before judging a company you don't recognize.
- **Outreach Writer:** follow the tone rules above.
